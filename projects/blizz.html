// 03 · Case Study

Blizz

A game of cat and mouse set in complete whiteout — the player navigates purely by sound. Solo-designed, sound-designed, and built in one week.

Play on itch.io ↗

Team
Solo
Role
Game Design, Sound Design (95% self-produced/recorded), Implementation
Timeframe
1 week
Tools
Godot

Overview

Blizz is a game of cat and mouse set in complete whiteout. The player navigates purely by sound: an unpleasant, dissonant sound marks the enemy to avoid, a harmonic sound marks the goal. Visibility is deliberately removed so that hearing becomes the only reliable sense.

Design Challenge

Sound isn't atmosphere layered on top here — it's the entire information channel the player navigates by. The design question throughout stayed simple: what does threat sound like, what does safety sound like, and how clearly can the two be told apart without an image to help?

  • Design a mechanic that lives entirely within a single sensory channel — hearing
  • Go from idea to a complete, playable loop solo within one week, with no scope creep and no incomplete systems
  • Make threat and safety instantly distinguishable by sound alone, with no visual reference to fall back on

My Contribution

Nearly all of the sound was self-recorded or self-produced. On one side sits the enemy sound, deliberately unnatural in tone. On the other sits a rain drum, producing a far-reaching, warm and harmonic sound that the player moves toward. Once the player finds its source and enters the igloo, the sound is dampened and the player is sheltered from the loud storm outside. If the enemy finds the player and gets closer, a dissonance filter activates and grows stronger the nearer it gets.

Outcome & Learnings

The atmosphere carries the entire feel of the game. Blizz proves two things at once: that a mechanic can live entirely within a single sensory channel, and that a complete, playable loop can go from idea to finished solo within one week — no scope creep, no incomplete systems.
